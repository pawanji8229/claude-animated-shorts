In 1968, a businessman bought an entire bridge from London and shipped it to the middle of the desert. But there wasn't even a river waiting for it. You see, London was replacing its old bridge, and an American businessman was building a new city in Arizona. But he needed something that would make people actually want to visit. So he spent 2.46 million dollars buying London Bridge. Workers carefully numbered more than ten thousand granite blocks, took the bridge apart, and loaded the stones onto ships. Across the ocean, through the Panama Canal, and finally arrived in Arizona. Then construction crews rebuilt the bridge around a modern concrete structure. But there was still one problem. There was no river. So they dug an entire channel underneath it. And in 1971, London Bridge reopened in the Arizona desert. Because he wasn't just buying a bridge. He was buying the world's attention.
