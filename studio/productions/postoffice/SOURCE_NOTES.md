# The Underwater Post Office — source notes

Facts used (web search, 2026-11-18; several pages could not be opened from the render container, so details come from
search-result summaries — cross-checked where two or more sources agree):

- Location: off Hideaway Island (Mele Island), a small resort island in Mele Bay, ~1 km off Efate near Port Vila,
  Vanuatu. The island is a marine sanctuary/reserve with coral reef and reef fish right off the beach.
- Opened 26 May 2003; billed as the world's first underwater post office, run with Vanuatu Post (own postcode).
- Depth about 3 m; roughly 50 m from shore (sources: 35 m – 50 m – "160 ft"). A built stairway/ladder leads into the water.
- Structure: a CONVERTED FIBREGLASS WATER TANK made by a local fibreglass firm; a gable roof was added and holes were cut
  for a counter on one side and an entrance on the other. Secured to the seabed with chains to rocks.
  A separate post box stands beside the tank for snorkellers.
- A (yellow) flag floats at the surface when the office is staffed. Staff are trained scuba divers; the office is
  manned for about an hour a day (longer on cruise-ship days).
- Postcards: special waterproof (plastic) postcards sold on the island. Mail is cancelled with a metal EMBOSSING device
  (ink would wash away / smear underwater). Cards are collected and enter the normal Vanuatu Post system (some cards
  receive a normal date stamp at the main post office in Port Vila).
- Story origin (anecdote): the idea came from one of the resort owners and the local postmaster.

Not verified (artistic choices, documented): exact tank colours and dimensions (modelled ~2.2 m diameter, ~2.1 m wall,
cream fibreglass with a blue-grey gable roof and algae growth), sign wording (generic "POST OFFICE" in-world sign; no
Vanuatu Post logo), the embosser model (a desk seal-embosser with a lever, cast frame, brass dies), postcard artwork
(original design), staff uniform colours.

Reef life used (typical of Vanuatu fringing reefs / Mele Bay): branching & table Acropora, massive Porites, brain coral,
plate Montipora, leather & soft corals, sea fans, giant clams, blue Linckia starfish, sea urchins, sea grass; fish: blue-green
chromis, sergeant majors (Abudefduf, famous for swarming snorkellers at Hideaway), convict surgeonfish, Moorish idol,
threadfin butterflyfish, anemonefish, bullethead parrotfish, yellowtail fusiliers.
