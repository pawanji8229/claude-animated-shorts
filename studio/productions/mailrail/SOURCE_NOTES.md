# Mail Rail: source notes (facts used, and creative licence)

## Facts used
- The Post Office Railway ("Mail Rail") opened in 1927 and closed in 2003. It was a 2 ft (610 mm) gauge, driverless, electric underground railway that carried letters and parcels between London sorting offices. Mount Pleasant was its hub; Paddington and Liverpool Street (among others) were on the line.
- The running tunnels were about 9 ft in diameter and lined with cast-iron segments. The trains were remotely controlled, with no driver and no passengers.
- The 1930s English Electric stock had a container well between motorised ends, so the mail travelled in containers. The trains had a centre conductor rail.

## Creative licence (original, not replicas)
- The train, stations, terraces and sorting office are original TwistFrame models inspired by the period. Proportions are simplified for 9:16 framing.
- The depth is compressed: the railway sits about 6.5 m below the street here, against roughly 21 m in reality, so that street and railway fit in one vertical frame.
- The distance between the two stations is shortened, a cinematic compression; the narration does not state a distance.
- The station names are used as in-world signage only.
