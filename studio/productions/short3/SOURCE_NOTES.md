# short3 — source notes (facts behind the visuals)

**Subject.** The 1930 move of the Indiana Bell Telephone Company building, Indianapolis. The story is widely reported in
engineering and local-history accounts. Details below are the commonly cited figures; the film shows the process
**generically** and never puts numbers or names on screen.

| fact (as commonly reported) | how the film uses it |
|---|---|
| eight-storey steel-frame building with brick and stone facing, roughly 11,000 tons | a tall, period brick and limestone office block with a cornice, a flagpole and many windows |
| the company needed the site for a bigger headquarters; demolition was rejected | an executive and a blueprint showing the new building's footprint overlapping the old one; a wrecking-ball crane arrives and is waved off |
| the building was jacked up onto timber cribbing and steel rollers | workers stack timber cribs and slide steel beams and rollers under the base |
| it was moved by jacks pushing against the base, a fraction of an inch at a time | rows of screw jacks turned by workers with long bars; the building creeps |
| it moved about 52 ft, was rotated about 90°, then moved about 100 ft | the path, shown by the building itself: slide, rotate on a pivot, slide again |
| about 600 employees kept working inside; utilities stayed connected with flexible lines | operators at a switchboard and clerks at desks keep working; flexible hoses and cables trail from the base; staff use a wooden gangway |
| the move took about a month | time passes through light changes and cloud motion, never with captions |

**Narration wording (ASR, confirm):**
- phrase 4 "you see **the** back in…" is read as "you see, back in…"
- phrase 11 "carefully **support of a** building" is read as "carefully supported the building"
- phrase 13 "moved it sideways hundred ninety degrees" is read as "moved it sideways, turned it ninety degrees"

The narration audio itself is never edited.

**Originality.** All characters, designs, sounds and staging are original. The building is a generic 1930 American
office block, not a replica of the real facade. In-world signage reads "TELEPHONE CO." only.
