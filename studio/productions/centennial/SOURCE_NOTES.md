# TwistFrame Lab #7: the Centennial Light (source notes)

These are the facts the visuals follow. They were checked on the web on 2026-11-19 against Wikipedia (Centennial Light), centennialbulb.org (timeline and facts), Atlas Obscura, NPR (2011) and FireRescue1.

## The object
- **Origin:** a hand-blown, carbon-filament incandescent bulb made by the Shelby Electric Company (Shelby, Ohio) in the late 1890s.
- **Brightness:** originally about 30–60 W. Today it glows very dimly, at about 4 W: a modest orange-gold filament glow, NOT a bright lamp.
- **Visual design:**
  - pear/teardrop glass envelope with the small sealed tip ("pip") at the bottom;
  - looped and coiled carbon filament on lead-in wires from a glass stem;
  - brass Edison screw base in a brass socket;
  - hangs from a cord.

## History used in the film
1. **1901:** Dennis Bernal (Livermore Power and Water Co.) donates the bulb. It goes up in the fire department's hose cart house in Livermore, California. Volunteer firefighters there used hand-drawn hose carts.
2. **Early 1900s:** it moves to the downtown station/garage used by fire and police. It survives the station's 1937 renovation.
   - *Simplification (stated):* the film shows ONE old station building from 1901 to 1976, dressed for each era. The narration only mentions the later move to "a new building".
3. **31 March 1976:** it moves to Fire Station #6, 4550 East Avenue, Livermore.
   - The cord was cut and the bulb travelled in a padded box with a fire-engine and police escort.
   - It was dark for about 22 minutes.
4. **20 May 2013:** its dedicated power supply failed.
   - A viewer watching the live "BulbCam" noticed the outage.
   - Firefighters bypassed the failed supply with an extension cord and the glow returned (outage reported as about 6 to 9¾ hours).
   - This is the film's "occasional interruption".
5. **Today:** a Centennial Light Bulb Committee looks after it (the fire department, Livermore Heritage Guild, LLNL and Sandia). A dedicated webcam streams it, the "BulbCam"; an earlier webcam famously wore out before the bulb did.
